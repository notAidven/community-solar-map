Why Community-Owned Solar?

• Access for All

Community solar lets renters, low-income households, and those with unsuitable roofs access solar benefits without rooftop panels.

• Lower Energy Bills

Subscribers typically save 5–15% annually through credits on their electricity bill.

• Grid Decarbonization

Projects displace fossil-fuel generation and contribute to state and federal clean energy mandates.

• Local Economic Benefits

Projects create local jobs and keep energy dollars circulating in communities.

About the Layers

Project Locations
Plots all community-owned solar projects as teal dots on the map.
Color by Ownership
Colors project dots by ownership type — green (cooperative), blue (publicly owned utility), purple (consumer-owned utility).
Color by Size
Scales dot size by MW-AC capacity, from smallest (<1 MW) to largest (17.5+ MW).
LMI Category
Shades states by their Low-to-Moderate Income community solar policy category.
High Level Findings
Shades states darker blue for each of 4 key programs they have adopted: Virtual Net Metering, a state CS program, CS-specific policy support, and CS grants.
Capacity Limits
Colors states by their community solar capacity limit policy category.

Key Terms Explained:

Community-Owned Solar
A shared solar installation whose output is credited to multiple subscribing households or businesses.
MW-AC / MW-DC
AC capacity is grid-delivered power after inverter conversion; DC is raw panel output. AC is typically 10–20% lower.
Interconnection Year
The year the project connected to the utility grid and began producing power for subscribers.
Ownership Type
Whether the project is run by a cooperative, rural electric co-op, publicly owned utility, or consumer-owned utility.
High Level Findings
A 0–4 score counting how many key community solar policies a state has adopted: Virtual Net Metering (VNM), a statewide CS program, CS-specific support policies, and CS grants. Darker blue = more programs.
